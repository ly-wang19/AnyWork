# Synthesize Sources

Produce an evidence-aware, directly usable work result. Keep facts, assumptions, and recommendations distinct.

## Workflow

1. Normalize the question, time period, definitions, and units across sources.
2. Build a claim-to-source matrix before writing conclusions.
3. Group supported findings into consensus, disagreement, and unknowns.
4. Explain whether conflicts come from data, definitions, timing, methods, or incentives.
5. Derive implications only when the evidence supports them.
6. Link every material conclusion back to its supporting source set.

## Deliver

- Scope and source set
- Consensus findings
- Disagreements and their causes
- Unknowns and evidence gaps
- Implications with traceability

## Quality gates

- Do not count repeated reporting of one origin as independent confirmation.
- Separate source statements from your synthesis and inference.

## Language

- Follow the user's latest explicit output-language instruction.
- Otherwise answer in the dominant language of the request: English, Simplified Chinese, or Japanese.
- Preserve quoted source text, code, identifiers, product names, and citations in their original language unless translation is requested.
- Match the audience's register; distinguish formal and conversational Chinese, business and plain Japanese, and the requested English variety.
